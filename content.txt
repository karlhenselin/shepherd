Ideas for more activities in Shepherd
=====================================

These are meant for this game as it already exists: Clover, Snowball, Biscuit, Milo; the 7×7 watercolor map; pasture at (2,2); quiet water at (5,4); hole bandage; night wolves; staff; fold as the gate. They fit either in the long walk before the pen, or as a second day after dawn — before (or instead of jumping straight to) “We will all be changed.”

Before the pen (stretch the first night)
---------------------------------------
1. After the staff, Milo is missing on purpose (now in the game). Keep that beat: nervous Milo hiding from howls. A second lost sheep at night would dilute it; instead, let Milo leave a wool snag on a thorn so the hint can show “he came this way.”
2. A dry creek bed between staff and fold. The flock refuses to cross until you find a shallow ford (a second quiet-water wash, smaller). Ties to “He leads me beside quiet waters” without repeating the first lake.
3. A lamb that cannot keep up. Carry it (tap to pick up, shepherd walks slower, one arm occupied so petting is off). Put it down at the gate. John 10 energy without a new verse.
4. Wolf tracks at the edge of the night veil. Not a fight: follow three prints to a ridge, see the wolf sprite at a distance, then it slinks off. Rewards noticing the new wolf art.
5. Campfire rest halfway to the fold. Sit (lie down briefly) so the flock huddles. Night baahs go quiet. If you skip it, Snowball is more likely to stray in the dark.
6. A broken rail on a small day-pen you pass. Repair with two sticks (already in the watercolor world as brown washes) so a stray cannot slip out while you bandage Biscuit.

A second day (Psalm 23:5–6, before the white robe)
--------------------------------------------------
7. Morning after the gate, do not fade to the mystery yet. The flock is hungry again. Lead them to a new pasture region (south of water) for “You prepare a table before me.” A flat rock with bread/cheese sprites; sheep eat while a wolf watches from the far side of the region and never enters.
8. Anointing: a clay jar of oil near the fold. Tap to anoint each sheep (and the shepherd last). Little gold glints, like Bible gems but warm. “You anoint my head with oil; my cup overflows.” Cup = the quiet water reflecting sunrise.
9. Goodness and mercy as two named sheep that follow *you* for the rest of the day — they never stray. Finding them is optional-looking but they appear on the path back from the table. Treasure chest can list them as companions, not gems.
10. Shade at noon. A single terebinth or broom tree in a hot washed-out region. Sheep overheat (slower, extra pant-baahs) until they stand in the shade. Short, readable care beat like thirst.
11. A neighbor’s stray mixed into your flock. Different tint, no name until you return them to a second fold on the opposite corner. Teaches “the one who enters by the gate” from the other side.
12. Count the flock at the gate every dusk. If the count is short, the hint points at who is missing. Makes Milo-style searches a habit, not a one-off.

Optional map toys (not story-critical)
--------------------------------------
13. More thorn thickets in the two unused mid-map bands (cols 0–1 and 6, rows 2–5) so freeing sheep is a regular care action, not a rare snag.
14. A Bible gem that only appears after you pet each named sheep once. Ties petting to the treasure chest.
15. Morning chores at the fold: fill a trough from the quiet water (slow walk with a sloshing jar), scatter straw, then the sheep will stay penned while you fetch one gem.
16. Eclipse / deeper valley: a second night that is only one region wide, music gone, wolves closer, staff required to keep follow distance tight. Then dawn and *then* 1 Corinthians 15:51.

What not to add
---------------
Combat with the wolf. Quizzes. A third identical “find a sheep” in daylight after Snowball — that is what made Milo skippable. If you want another find, make it night, named, and required for the gate, like Milo is now.
